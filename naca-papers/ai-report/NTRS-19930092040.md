## 1. Why this paper

Chosen as the first "computational archaeology" target because it is the purest
instance of the thesis: a self-contained mathematical problem whose *complete*
solution was blocked not by theory but by machine time. The paper's own closing
paragraph says so directly — further solutions "should be performed as soon as
more high-speed computing-machine service can be obtained." This report is the
receipt for that service, delivered 76 years later.

The physical question is the stability of a free shear layer (two parallel
streams, one at rest, U₂/U₁ = 0) — the canonical mixing layer, then an open
problem with no solid-boundary analogue to lean on.

## 2. What the paper did (1948–49)

- **Base flow:** similarity solution of `f·f″ + 2f‴ = 0` (eq. 3), integrated
  step-by-step via Taylor-series expansion (eq. 20–22), started from an
  asymptotic expansion whose coefficients the appendix tabulates to 8 digits
  (a = 1.23849316 …).
- **Stability:** the Orr–Sommerfeld equation (eq. 13) solved *indirectly* —
  expanding φ in powers of (−i/αR) and keeping **two terms**, then shooting
  along a straight-line path through the complex plane (interval w = 0.2+0.05i)
  to dodge the critical-layer singularity at f′ = c.
- **Machine:** the IBM Selective Sequence Electronic Calculator, time donated
  by IBM; eigenvalues (α, c, R) hunted by "trial-and-error" until R came out real.
- **Honest limits, stated by the author:** the expansion is invalid at low αR,
  so *"it was impossible to obtain the lower branch of the curve of neutral
  stability."* Results: flow unstable except at very low R; inviscid (inflection-
  point) instability persists as R→∞; fig. 3 gives the neutral curve's upper
  branch plus Im c = ±0.05, +0.10 contours.

## 3. What we tested, and how

Three claims, one extension:

1. **Reproducibility:** do the paper's base-flow coefficients and profile hold
   up numerically? → Integrated eq. 3 seeded from the paper's *own appendix
   series*; far-field decay slope 0.6167 vs paper's a/2 = 0.6192 (0.4% — the gap
   is exactly the amplification of their 8-digit rounding); f′(+30) → 1 to 2 ppm.
2. **Their conclusions:** unstable at all but low R; inflection-point (Rayleigh)
   mechanism; neutral curve + equal-growth contours.
3. **The extension they couldn't do:** solve the *full* Orr–Sommerfeld equation
   directly — no expansion, no complex-plane path — and recover the missing
   lower branch of the neutral curve.

**Method mapping:**

| | Lessen (1950) | This run |
|---|---|---|
| Base flow | Taylor-series stepping from appendix series | Runge–Kutta (solve_ivp, rtol 1e-11) seeded from the **same appendix series** |
| Stability eq. | 2-term expansion in (−i/αR) + complex-plane shooting | Direct generalized eigenproblem Aφ = cBφ, full OS operator |
| Discretization | — (ODE shooting, w = 0.2+0.05i) | Chebyshev collocation, sinh-mapped domain, N = 160 (filter grid N = 136) |
| Spurious modes | interval-halving checks | two-grid convergence filter (modes must agree across N to 5×10⁻⁵) |
| Eigenvalue search | trial-and-error until Im R = 0 | QZ on all modes at once; bracket + bisection for α_s |
| Inviscid limit | expansion as αR → ∞ | Rayleigh equation solved directly (viscous term dropped) |
| Hardware | IBM SSEC, months of donated time | 4 CPU cores, 299 s |

## 4. Results

| Quantity | Value | vs. paper |
|---|---|---|
| Inviscid cut-off α_s | **0.3050** (c_s = 0.5651) | paper defines α_s but reports no number — this fills it in |
| Max amplification Im c | **0.186** at (α=0.2, R=10⁴) | paper's contours stop at +0.10 |
| Lowest unstable R | **≤ 5** (sweep floor) | "unstable except for very low Reynolds numbers" — confirmed emphatically |
| Neutral curve | full curve incl. **lower branch to R = 5** | the branch the paper explicitly could not obtain |
| Convergence | Δc ≤ 7×10⁻⁷ (interior), 1.8×10⁻⁵ (near cut-off) | paper's own standard was 5 significant figures by interval halving |

Independent sanity check: Michalke's tanh-layer analogue predicts α_s ≈ 0.34
for this profile's vorticity thickness — we land within 12%, the expected level
of agreement between two different profile families.

All three of the paper's stated conclusions verify. The one thing we could not
cross-check numerically is the paper's fig. 3 curve *values* themselves — its
tables did not survive OCR (figure-only), so agreement is at the level of
topology (band shape, contour levels ±0.05/+0.10, R ≈ 10–10³ range) rather
than digit-for-digit overlay.

## 5. How it applies

- **The thesis, demonstrated end-to-end:** a 1950 computation bounded by
  machine access becomes a 5-minute CPU job whose bottleneck is writing the
  code, not running it. The interesting output is not "we confirmed Lessen" —
  it is that the *missing half of his result* falls out for free once the
  equation is solved without his asymptotic workaround. The lower branch
  matters physically: it says the mixing layer has essentially no viscous
  critical Reynolds number, i.e. it is the most unstable canonical flow —
  the reason it became the standard testbed for transition research.
- **Method transplant:** the paper's asymptotic series, built to *start* an
  integration in 1948, is reused here for the same purpose against a modern
  integrator — the 1950 mathematics is not obsolete, it is load-bearing.
- **Template:** this run establishes the per-paper pattern for the remaining
  corpus entries (`sim/<id>.py`, `sbatch/<id>.sh`, `results/<id>/`,
  `ai-report/<id>.md`), including the failure-driven engineering notes below.

## 6. Engineering notes (what the agent had to fix)

For honesty about the process — every one of these is a 1948-flavoured obstacle
in modern dress: (1) module-chain discovery (scipy-stack/2026b requires
StdEnv/2026); (2) shooting the base flow is exponentially ill-conditioned on
the resting side — fixed by reusing the paper's series; (3) over-refinement
destroys collocation spectra ((D²)² roundoff at N=200+ — the fix is *fewer*
points with a sinh map); (4) the Rayleigh limit needs 2 boundary conditions,
not the viscous problem's 4 — over-imposing kills the unstable pair; (5) a
scipy API detail (eig returns eigenvalues *and* eigenvectors by default).
Total: 11 Slurm submissions, ~25 minutes of cluster time including all failures.
