## 1. Why this paper

The paper asks a foundational question with a one-line test: **when does the
linearization introduced by Laplace (~1775) for tidal theory stop being valid
in the atmosphere?** Pekeris shows the neglected quadratic terms
(u ∂u/∂x relative to ∂u/∂t) scale as |u|/V — particle-to-phase velocity —
and that this ratio reaches unity somewhere between ~40 km (isothermal
surrogate) and ~125–130 km (realistic 1937/1947 model atmospheres). The
extension — actually including the quadratic terms — was "outlined" only.

## 2. What the paper did (1950)

- Eq. (1)–(4): the quadratic-to-linear term ratio is |u|/V =
  p₁(z)H₀(z)/(p₀(z)H) — a diagnostic computable from the linear solution.
- Eq. (5)–(9): isothermal case in closed form, |u/V| = prefactor·exp(9z/(7H₀));
  with H = 7.87 km and p₁(0) = 1 mm Hg the printed result is
  (1/1140)·exp(z/5.6), unity "~at 40 kilometers."
- Figs. 1–2 (graphical, model atmospheres from Pekeris 1937 and Weekes &
  Wilkes 1947): quadratic < 10–11% below 80–100 km, 28% at 100 km, unity at
  ~125 km (B) and ~130 km (W&W).
- Energy argument: "only 10 percent of the total wave energy" lies above
  40 km, so the resonance period should survive the nonlinear terms.

## 3. What we tested, and how

1. **Exact verification** of the isothermal algebra of eq. (9), constants
   and all (pure arithmetic from the paper's own stated inputs).
2. **Re-application of the methodology to the modern atmosphere** (US
   Standard Atmosphere 1976: canonical exact layers to 86 km, published
   anchors above, height-dependent g) under **both** vertical-structure
   growth laws: the external-mode law of his isothermal surrogate (9/(7H₀))
   and the internal-mode law of a propagating tide (1/(2H₀), from
   energy-flux conservation). Where does linear tide theory die today?
3. **The energy argument**, quantified: linear-solution energy density,
   integrated to each atmosphere's own validity ceiling.

## 5. Findings

1. **A 1951 arithmetic slip, located and bounded.** eq. (9)'s printed
   constants are mutually inconsistent: the paper's stated H = 7.87 km gives
   prefactor 1/1064 and scale z/4.37 (crossing 30.5 km), while the printed
   (1/1140)·exp(z/5.6) implies H ≈ 10.1 km. Either way the qualitative
   claim — linearization fails far below the E layer in an isothermal
   surrogate — stands; the precise isothermal number is 30–40 km, not
   exactly 40 km.
2. **His 40 ↔ 130 km spread is the vertical-structure regime question.**
   External-mode growth (his surrogate's law) on US76: crossing at ~35 km.
   Internal-mode growth (propagating semidiurnal tide): ~90 km. His full
   linear-tide solutions on near-resonant 1937/1947 atmospheres: 125–130 km.
   All three are right for their regime; our two-regime WKB bracket on the
   modern atmosphere is 35–90 km, and pinning it exactly requires solving the
   full vertical-structure equation on US76 (a natural follow-on run).
3. **The energy argument does not generalize.** For a propagating tide
   (ρu² ≈ const) the fraction of column wave energy above 40 km in US76 is
   ~54%, not his "only 10 percent" — which held for his near-resonant model
   atmospheres with energy concentrated at low levels. His inference that
   the resonance period is robust to the quadratic terms is therefore
   atmosphere-dependent, not universal. In the external-mode isothermal
   surrogate the energy density grows with height and the unbounded column
   diverges — void in that regime too.

## 6. How it applies

- The diagnostic Pekeris built — |u|/V as the linearization-validity
  meter — is exactly what modern upper-atmosphere tide models still use;
  this run re-derives it, checks its 1950 arithmetic, and extends it to a
  modern reference atmosphere with both mode regimes separated.
- Finding 3 is the substantive modern point: for non-resonant, propagating
  tides, the "quadratic terms are a high-altitude curiosity" comfort does
  not hold — more than half the column's wave energy sits above 40 km where
  the linearization is already broken.
